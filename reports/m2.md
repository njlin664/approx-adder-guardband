## Verdict

At the same critical path delay,LOA gives about 2.9 times lower MSE for aging budget (LOA-3 at 4 against TruA-2 at 
11.5) and 4.5 times lower for the 70 C budget (LOA-7 at 1024 against TruA-6 at 4652). Both designs shorten the exact 
carry chain to the same length, but truncation throws the low bits away, while LOA keeps their OR and one guessed 
carry, so the same speed buys less error.

## Limitations

Uniform derating: one factor (×1.08, ×1.37) applies to every gate. The paper used degradation-aware libraries where each cell ages differently,
and the critical path itself can shift.
Borrowed factors: the factors come from the paper’s technology, applied to a 45 nm open library.
Open-source flow: Yosys, ABC and OpenSTA, not Synopsys Design Compiler and PrimeTime. One library, one corner, no wire delays, and assumed
input drive and output load.
Ripple-carry baseline: a faster exact adder might remove the need to approximate at all. Day 15 tests this: The fast-adder check below tests this.
Scope: delay and error only. No power analysis, and 16-bit adders only.
Counterpoint: truncation is the smallest design (about 9% less area than LOA at 70 °C), and segmentation has the lowest error rate.
“Best” depends on which metric you weight.
Compared with the paper: the paper needs TruA-5 or LOA-6 for aging and TruA-9 or LOA-10 for 70 °C. Here the picks need 2 or 3 bits, and 6 or 7.
A likely reason is that accurate adder is a plain ripple-carry chain, where delay falls almost in proportion to bits removed. 
A faster exact adder would gain less per bit, so more bits would need to go.


Is a faster exact adder the better fix? A faster exact adder does remove the guard-band if the clock was set by a ripple-carry adder: the fast 
exact adder is 296 ps, and even at x1.37 it needs only 406 ps, well inside 528.5 ps, with zero error. It costs about 1.9x the area of 
LOA-7 (121.8 against 63.0 square micrometres). But once the fast adder sets the clock, aging and heat slow it down too, the guard-band problem 
comes back at 296 ps, and LOA again beats truncation (TruA-9 against LOA-10 at 70 C).

Image-quality check (5x5 blur, mean PSNR over 10 images, 30 dB target): with ripple adders, every TruA and LOA pick passes 30 dB. With fast adders at 70 C, the timing picks are TruA-9 and LOA-10 (the same as the paper's Table IV), and neither reaches 30 dB: truncation gets 21.1 dB, LOA gets 29.3 dB, just short of the target. So LOA is about 8 dB better, with 4.7 times lower MSE, but the application target is only met by the milder slowdown cases. ESA fails in every case.
With fast adders and mild 10-year aging, TruA-3 (MSE 59.5, 56.0 dB) and LOA-5 (MSE 64, 54.3 dB) are essentially tied and both pass 30 dB comfortably, so the difference between the two families only becomes decisive at large slowdowns.
